# Story shapes

What has worked for a 3 to 5 minute demo. These are starting points for the proposal in step 2; the
owner decides the story.

## The shape

1. **The problem, in the user's terms** (15 to 30 s). One `TitleCard` or `Statement`, or a recording
   of the tool they use today. No architecture yet.
2. **The product doing the job** (the largest part, 1.5 to 3 minutes). `ScreenRecording` scenes of
   the real flow, in the order a user meets it. One idea per scene.
3. **The proof** (45 to 90 s). What makes the claim believable: the gate going red and green
   (`Terminal`), the numbers (`MetricCards`), the trace or the dashboard (`ScreenRecording`), the
   key piece of code (`CodeHighlight`).
4. **The close** (5 to 15 s). `EndCard` with where to find the work.

For work whose point is a decision rather than a product (a platform, an audit, a migration), swap 2
and 3: problem, the key decision and why, the thing running, the proof, the close.

## Numbers that hold

- Speech runs at about 150 words a minute. Four minutes is about 600 words; write fewer.
- A line is one or two sentences. A scene with more than three lines is two scenes.
- A recording that holds on a still screen for more than a few seconds loses the viewer: split it, or
  speed it up within the limit, or cut it.
- A scene shorter than about three seconds reads as a flash.

## Pacing

Three options for the proposal; the owner picks one, and the storyboard's `min_seconds` and the
lines follow it. Each keeps the reading floor: 0.3 s a word of on-screen text, 0.8 s for a label,
counted from the moment the whole text is in; a line comes in fast and then holds.

| Option | Scenes in 4 minutes | Holds | Where it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brisk | 18 to 24, 8 to 14 s each | one line a scene; a card holds 3 to 4 s | a product with many small moments; a viewer who knows the field |
| Measured | 12 to 16, 12 to 20 s each | two lines a scene; a card holds 4 to 6 s | the usual demo: one idea a scene, with room to land |
| Unhurried | 8 to 11, 20 to 30 s each | up to three lines; a card holds 6 to 8 s | a decision or a migration, where the why takes words |

Whichever option: the same pace throughout, except the close, which slows. A sequence of cards or
labels is revealed quickly and then held as a set, never one flash after another.

## Writing the lines

- State facts. Short sentences. One claim each.
- Say what is on screen, or what it means; never read the screen aloud.
- A key phrase over a recording is three to five words, and it is the owner's wording.
- Leave room: the last line of a scene should end before the scene's point has finished landing.

## Before proposing

List what the brief says the video must show, and put each item against a scene. An item with no
scene is a gap to raise, not to hide.
